Weapon Animation — Integration Matrix

The clip system is additive: it never replaces the procedural pipeline, and it only stands a procedural layer down where the two would animate the same thing. This page is the exact behaviour of each layer, with and without a clip.

The authority rule

Gameplay owns timing; animation adapts to it. Fire rate, reloadDuration, ammo refill moments and the de-equip wait stay owned by the existing systems. Clips change their playback speed to match — never the other way round. This is why a reload clip authored at 2.4 s still lands exactly on a 1.8 s reloadDuration, and why changing reloadDuration at runtime immediately retimes the next reload.

Matrix

Procedural layer No clip in the matching slot Clip present
Equip tween (equipStartingPos/Rot, LeanTween) Runs exactly as before. Suppressed for this weapon. equipPos/equipQuaternion are explicitly zeroed and the Equip clip owns the draw. The equip SFX still plays.
De-equip tween (equipEndingPos/Rot) Runs as before; the switch-out waits max(posEndTime, rotEndTime). Suppressed. The switch-out instead waits HolsterDuration (clip length ÷ holster speed multiplier), so the model is never hidden mid-animation. The de-equip SFX still plays.
Reload tilt (AFPS_WeaponTilt, enableReloadingTilt) Runs as before on the basic locomotion stances. Stands down. The clip already animates the hands; tilting the root underneath would drag the authored pose off-centre. All other tilt stances are unaffected.
Weapon bob Unchanged. Unchanged, except it is scaled by Procedural ▸ Weight During Reload / Inspect while those clips play (default 1 = untouched).
Weapon sway Unchanged. As bob.
Movement tilt (non-reload stances) Unchanged. As bob.
Recoil (AFPS_RecoilController) Unchanged. Unchanged — recoil and the Fire clip are driven from the same emission moment, so they stay in sync by construction. Also scaled by the procedural weight.
Weapon bump Unchanged. Unchanged. Never scaled — it is a collision response, not a flourish.
ADS (position, rotation, FOV, spread) Unchanged. Unchanged. Aiming additionally selects the Fire (ADS) clip over Fire when that slot is filled, feeds the IsADS parameter, and suppresses the Sprint / Slide / Wallrun states (see below).
Shared movement arms (AFPS_ArmsController) Unchanged. Unchanged, but the weapon rig's own arm renderers hide for exactly the window in which the movement arms are forced visible (climb / ledge hang / mantle), so you never see four arms.

The Procedural weights are the one place the two systems deliberately interact. They ease animationProceduralMultiplier between 1 and the configured value over Procedural Weight Blend Time, and AFPS_WeaponSystem multiplies the grouped recoil + sway + bob + tilt term by it. Left at 1 — the default — the transform composition is bit-identical to a weapon without this component.

Per-slot behaviour

Slot Plays when Timing
Idle Base layer default; whenever nothing else drives locomotion. Loops at authored speed.
Walk Speed above 0.2 m/s. Loops; 0.15 s blend from Idle.
Sprint IsSprinting (MovementState.Sprinting) and not aiming. Loops; 0.15 s blend from Walk. Aiming mid-sprint blends straight back to Walk.
Slide IsSliding (MovementState.Sliding) and not aiming, from anywhere in the locomotion chain. Loops for the length of the slide; 0.15 s blend in, and out to whichever of Idle/Walk/Sprint the player lands in.
Wallrun IsWallrunning (MovementState.Wallrunning) and not aiming, from anywhere in the locomotion chain. As Slide. The Actions layer stays free, so firing and reloading animate normally mid-wallrun.
Swim Idle IsSwimming and no directional input — treading water. Loops. Also the fallback for any directional swim slot you leave empty.
Swim Forward IsSwimming and forward input. Loops; 0.15 s blend.
Swim Backward IsSwimming and backward input. Loops; 0.15 s blend.
Swim Up IsSwimming and the jump/ascend button held. Wins over forward/back. Loops; 0.15 s blend.
Swim Down IsSwimming and the crouch/descend button held. Wins over forward/back. Loops; 0.15 s blend.
Equip Every draw: number key, cycle, scroll, last-weapon, holster-redraw, pickup-equip, and the legacy scene-child auto-equip. Authored speed, or squeezed into the weapon's equip settings times with Scale Equip To Settings.
Fire Each actual shot emission — once per burst bullet, at the true moment for delayed fire scripts. Never on a dry fire. Restarts from frame 0 every shot, so a fire rate faster than the clip retriggers cleanly.
Fire (ADS) As Fire, while the weapon's ADS script reports aiming. As Fire. Falls back to Fire when this slot is empty.
Reload The designated ammo pool starts reloading. Speed = clip length ÷ reloadDuration when Sync Reload To Ammo Duration is on (default).
Reload (Empty Mag) As Reload, when the mag is at 0. As Reload. Falls back to Reload when empty.
Inspect After Inspect After Idle Seconds of genuine idle, via PlayInspect(), or via an Inspect input action if you add one. Authored speed. Interrupted by any fire, reload, aim or switch.
Holster Every switch-out and the holster key. Plays at Holster Speed Multiplier; the switch-out wait matches.

Swimming

IsSwimming is set while the player is in a fluid volume whose Fluid Movement Type is Default or Swim. NoSwim volumes apply ordinary land movement (wading), so they animate as land movement — no swim state is entered.

Which of the five poses plays is chosen from input, not velocity: buoyancy drifts the player up and down on its own, so a velocity-derived choice would flicker between ascending and descending while they float in place. Vertical intent wins over forward/back — holding ascend is a deliberate act and the more distinctive pose.

The five states hang off Any State on IsSwimming AND SwimDirection == n, so changing direction needs no state-to-state transitions; the matching entry simply wins. Leaving a directional slot empty falls back to Swim Idle rather than popping into an empty state, and if Swim Idle is empty too the whole swim set is reported off and the land locomotion chain takes over.

Firing, reloading and equipping all animate normally while swimming — those live on the Actions layer, which the Base layer never touches.

Aiming suppresses the whole-body poses

Sprint, Slide and Wallrun all take the hands off the sights, so the Base layer will not enter them while the player is aiming — and aiming during one exits it back to Walk. Without this, a sprint clip would drop the weapon off the shoulder while the procedural ADS pose was still holding the root on target, and the two would visibly fight.

Idle and Walk are unaffected: aiming while walking is normal, and a walk clip authored for an FPS keeps the weapon up.

The swim states are deliberately not suppressed. Sprint, slide and wallrun are brief bursts where freezing to the aim pose reads fine; swimming is a sustained mode, and holding a land idle pose for the length of a swim while the player is visibly moving through water looks far worse. If you would rather it behaved like the others, add an IsADS condition to the Any State entries in your copy of the controller.

This lives in the template graph, not in code — the conditions are the IsADS clauses on the Walk→Sprint, Any State→Slide and Any State→Wallrun transitions. If you have authored an aimed sprint or slide, delete those clauses in your own copy of the controller and the states become reachable again.

Empty locomotion slots

Locomotion parameters are only reported for slots that actually hold a clip. A weapon with just an Idle clip reports Speed as 0 and never raises IsSprinting, IsSliding, IsWallrunning or IsSwimming — so it stays in Idle instead of transitioning into an empty state, which would snap the rig to its default pose.

If you replaced those states' motions directly in a duplicated controller (a walk blend tree, say) rather than using the clip slots, turn off Locomotion ▸ Gate Locomotion To Filled Slots so the raw values reach your graph.

Weapons this does not change

  • Weapons with no AFPS_WeaponAnimator at all.
  • Weapons with the component but an empty override controller.
  • AI-held weapons (no AFPS_WeaponSystem): fire and reload still animate; movement, ADS and arms-visibility inputs are simply absent and their parameters stay at their defaults.
  • Old-style useArms weapons sharing the movement arms: fully supported. A weapon-only rig just has an empty arm-renderer list.

Multiple ammo pools

A weapon with more than one AFPS_WeaponAmmo (e.g. an underbarrel launcher) drives the reload clip from one designated pool, chosen by Reload ▸ Reload Pool Index in component order. The other pools reload normally and animate nothing, which is deliberate: a launcher reload should not crossfade the rifle's hand animation. Per-pool clip mapping is a documented extension, not built in — see Extending the system.
