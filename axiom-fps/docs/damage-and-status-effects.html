Damage Types and Status Effects

Damage in Axiom FPS has an identity, and that identity can carry consequences. A hit does not just subtract health: it names what kind of damage it was, which decides how the UI colours it, how the death screen words it, what the target resists, and what lingering effects it leaves behind.

Both halves are ScriptableObject assets, so new damage kinds and new effects are authored in the Inspector rather than written in code.

Damage types

A damage kind is an AFPS_DamageType asset. It replaces the old fixed DamageType enum, which means you can add your own without editing the asset's source.

Ten presets ship under Presets/Health System/Damage Types/: Ballistic, Electric, Environment, Explosive, Fall, Fire, Generic, Ice, Melee, and Poison.

Each asset carries:

Field What it does
Display Name The name UI shows — damage numbers, killfeed. Falls back to the asset name when blank.
Color Tint used by UI that colours damage by type, such as floating damage numbers.
Icon Optional sprite for killfeed and other UI.
Death Message Noun The noun dropped into the death screen's "killed by …" line — "an explosion", "the fall". Falls back to the lowercased display name.
Apply On Hit Status effects applied to the target automatically on a direct hit of this type — Fire applying Burn, for instance.

Damage types are referenced from DamageInfo.type, from resistance entries on AFPS_Health, from weapon and explosion settings, and from status effects themselves.

Note

Apply On Hit only fires on a direct hit. A status effect's own ticks do not re-trigger it, so a Burn tick will never re-apply Burn and refresh its own timer indefinitely.

Status effects

A status effect is an AFPS_StatusEffect asset — a template. At runtime AFPS_Health.GiveNewStatusEffect instantiates a live StatusEffect from it, so one asset can be active on many targets, and stack on a single target, without them sharing state.

Over twenty presets ship under Presets/Health System/Status Effects/, including Burn, Bleed, Poison, Cold, Frozen, Stun, Blind, Flashbang, Knockback, Haste, Slow, and Regeneration.

Timing and impact

Build Up Max is how much the effect must accumulate before it applies at all — the "you are getting cold" phase before Cold actually lands. Reapply Time is the tick interval, and Apply Time is how long the effect lasts once applied.

Each tick moves Health Impact and Overheal Impact, either of which may be negative or positive — that is the difference between Poison and Regeneration. The effect's own Damage Type decides which of the target's resistances and vulnerabilities apply to those ticks; leave it null for untyped. Max Stacks caps how many copies can sit on one target, with 0 or 1 meaning no stacking.

Three optional sounds play at the target's position: on apply, on every reapply tick, and on end. Be careful with the reapply sound on a short tick interval.

Behaviours

Anything an effect does other than move health is a behaviour — a separate asset in the effect's Behaviours list. They are applied when the effect starts, ticked on each reapply, and undone when it ends. An effect with an empty list is a pure health-over-time effect.

Behaviour What it does
Move Speed Scales the target's movement speed while active. Registers a multiplier on AFPS_Movement keyed by the effect instance, so multiple slows stack and each cleans up its own.
Control Lock Locks movement, look, and firing in any combination — everything for a Stun, movement only for a root or snare, firing only for a disarm.
Knockback Launches the target's Rigidbody away from whoever applied the effect, plus an upward kick, and strips control until they land again.
Screen Effect Shows a full-screen overlay on the local player while active — frost creeping in at the edges, fire, blind/flashbang whiteout. Driven through AFPS_StatusScreenEffects.
VFX Spawns a world-space prefab on the target for the effect's duration and destroys it at the end — fire on a burning enemy, frost on a chilled one. It follows the character.

Behaviours key their state to the specific runtime effect instance rather than to the asset, which is what lets each stack register and clean up independently.

Chaining and cancelling

Next Status Effect applies a fresh effect when this one expires. Cold becoming Frozen is the shipped example.

Cancels Effects lists effect assets that this one removes on application, and suppresses for as long as it stays active — a Wet effect naming Burn to douse a fire and stop it re-igniting. Cancels Damage Types does the same thing by damage type instead, for "water cancels all fire" without naming each fire effect individually.

The control lock

Stuns, knockbacks, and anything else that takes control away share one reference-counted lock, AFPS_PlayerControlLock, covering movement, look, and firing independently.

Reference counting is the point. Two overlapping stuns used to fight each other — whichever ended first handed control back while the other was still running. Each holder now takes its own reference and releases only that one, so control returns when the last holder lets go. The knockback behaviour additionally releases its lock the moment the player lands, rather than on a fixed timer.

Attribution

Status-effect damage credits the original attacker, not the effect. A target that burns to death from your rocket's Fire gives you the kill, and the death screen and floating damage numbers agree. See Death and Respawn for the kill record this feeds.
