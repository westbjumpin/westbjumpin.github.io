Death and Respawn

Out of the box the player respawns instantly at the last checkpoint. Adding an AFPS_DeathController to the player replaces that with a full death sequence — and removing the component puts the old behaviour back. Nothing else changes either way, so existing scenes are unaffected until you opt in.

Everything about the sequence lives on a preset: which camera takes over, how long it holds, what the screen says, and where you come back.

Setup

  1. Add AFPS Death Controller to the player root — the object with AFPS_Health. Do it on the AFPS Player prefab, not a scene copy. Adding it auto-wires the references it can find on the player.
  2. Assign a preset from Assets/Axiom FPS/Presets/Death/ to its Settings field. That alone gives a working death sequence, with no on-screen text.
  3. Optional, for on-screen text. Add a child GameObject with a UIDocument whose Source Asset is UI Toolkit/DeathScreen/DeathScreen.uxml and whose Panel Settings is the shipped DeathScreenPanelSettings asset in the same folder. Add AFPS Death Screen (UI Toolkit) beside it, then drag that component into the controller's Death Screen field. Canvas HUD projects use AFPS Death Screen (UGUI) instead. Keep the GameObject active — the screen hides itself.
  4. Optional. Drop your HUD objects into Hide While Dead.
  5. Press the controller's Debug: Kill Player button in play mode to preview the sequence without hunting for something to die to.

Shipped presets

Under Presets/Death/:

Preset What you get
Flat You Died First-person view, "YOU DIED", respawn button.
Freezecam Holds the frame you died on, slow dolly out, 3s countdown then a button.
Deathcam Killcam framing whoever killed you, corpse in frame, automatic respawn at a spawn point.
Orbit Cinematic Slow orbit around the body, holds until you ask to respawn.

Customising

Everything is on the preset asset: timing, screen text, time dilation, cursor and input policy. The two slots that change the most are:

Camera Mode (Presets/Death/Cameras/) — Keep First Person, Freeze Cam, Follow Body, Killer Cam, or Orbit. Subclass AFPS_DeathCameraMode to write your own.

Respawn Strategy (Presets/Death/Respawn/) — Checkpoint (the original behaviour), Spawn Point (needs an AFPS_SpawnPointRegistry in the scene), Reload Scene, or Event Only for your own respawn code.

Screen text supports the {killer}, {damageType}, {distance}, {damage} and {seconds} tokens, filled from the kill record described below.

Bodies and ragdolls

Any camera that leaves first person needs something to look at. Add an AFPS Death Body Handler to the player and choose: keep in place, hide the body, spawn a corpse prefab, or enable a ragdoll you authored on your own model. The killing blow's impulse is applied either way.

Kill records

Every death — player, AI, or a destructible prop — raises an AFPS_KillInfo record through the victim's AFPS_Health.OnKilled event and the static AFPS_Health.OnAnyKilled. Both are empty hooks by default; subscribe to drive a killfeed, scoring, on-death loot, AI aggro, or achievements without touching the health system.

The record names the victim and their AFPS_Agent, the GameObject credited with the kill, the killer's agent, the AFPS_DamageType of the killing blow, and flags for headshots and self-inflicted deaths. It is built from the victim's latched last damage, so a death from your rocket's Burn credits you rather than the fire — the same attribution the death screen and floating damage numbers use.

Two cases are worth knowing about:

  • Unattributed deaths. A fall, drowning, or a scripted kill has no instigator. hasKiller is false and the killer fields stay empty. This is not counted as self-inflicted, because there is nothing to credit.
  • Self-inflicted deaths. Your own explosive, your own fire, or an explosive barrel you shot resolves back to you, and selfInflicted is true. Explosive props credit correctly through chain reactions — see the Firing Range barrels.
